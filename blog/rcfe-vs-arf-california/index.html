RCFE vs ARF in California: What's the Difference?

October 2026 · RCFE ARF · By the Title22 team

Both are licensed by CDSS Community Care Licensing. Both give nonmedical care and supervision, around the clock, in a home-like setting. The difference starts with who lives there, and from there almost every rule has its own version.

Who lives there

An Adult Residential Facility (ARF) provides 24-hour nonmedical care and supervision to adults 18 through 59 (Title 22, section 80001). Residents often live with developmental, physical or mental health disabilities. Some are clients of one of California's 21 regional centers, and the ARF rules have staffing terms written for them (section 85065.5).

An ARF can admit or keep someone 60 or older only if their needs are compatible with the other residents and they need the same level of care. In an ARF with more than six beds, if residents 60 and older would be more than 25 percent of the residents, the licensee needs an exception from CDSS (section 85068.4).

A Residential Care Facility for the Elderly (RCFE) is a housing arrangement chosen voluntarily by the resident, where 75 percent of residents are 60 or older. Younger residents must have needs compatible with the others (section 87101).

What both administrators share

  • At least 21 years old, with a high school diploma or GED (CDSS FAQ).
  • Initial training from an ACB-approved vendor (list of approved vendors). Unless the rules say otherwise, hours must be in person or live-stream.
  • The exam must be passed within 60 days of finishing the training, with up to three attempts. Passing is 70 percent and the fee is $100 (exam rules).
  • Apply within 30 days of the email saying you passed. The application fee is $140 (initial procedures).
  • Renew no more than 90 days before your certificate expires. Late renewals add a $300 delinquency fee. A certificate expired more than four years cannot be renewed, and you start over with the initial training (renewal procedures).
  • CDSS will not count more than 10 hours of CE completed in a single day.

One certificate does not cover the other. CDSS says an applicant "must meet the qualifications for each program separately" (initial procedures).

ARF administrators have one extra rule worth knowing: for a home with 7 to 15 residents, the administrator needs at least one year of work experience in residential care (section 85064).

Which one do I need?

Start with who you plan to serve. A home mostly for older adults is an RCFE. A home for younger adults with disabilities is an ARF. If you are unsure, call your Regional Office before you apply, because the license type decides your rules, your training and your inspection checklist.
