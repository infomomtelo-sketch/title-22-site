Staff File Checklist for a New California Care Home: RCFE and ARF

October 2026 · RCFE ARF · By the Title22 team

You have your license and your first caregivers. Before a Licensing Program Analyst ever opens a drawer, each person who works in the home needs a file, and the rules say what goes in it. This guide lists those rules for both license types, with the section each one comes from.

It covers staff files only. Resident records are a separate set of rules and are not in this article.

Who needs a file

  • The licensee, the administrator and every employee. Both license types say so in the first line of their personnel records rule (section 87412(a) for RCFEs, section 80066(a) for ARFs).
  • Every volunteer, with a shorter file (sections 87412(b) and 80066(b)).

Start with your license type. An RCFE follows Chapter 8 of Title 22 (the 87000 sections). An ARF follows Chapter 1 and Chapter 6 (the 80000 and 85000 sections). The two lists look alike but are not the same. If you are not sure which you are, read RCFE vs ARF in California first.

RCFE: what goes in each staff file

Section 87412(a) lists thirteen items for the licensee, the administrator and each employee:

  • Full name
  • Social Security number
  • Date of employment
  • Written proof the person is at least 18, such as a copy of a birth certificate or driver's license
  • Home address and telephone number
  • Educational background. For an administrator, proof of the education the administrator rules require
  • Past experience, including the types of work and former employers
  • The type of position held
  • Termination date, if the person no longer works there
  • Reasons for leaving
  • A health screening (see the timing below)
  • Hazardous health condition documents, as section 87411 describes
  • For anyone who must be fingerprinted: a signed statement about their criminal record history, and proof of a criminal record clearance or exemption

For a certified administrator, a copy of the current, valid administrator certificate meets the criminal record item (section 87412(a)(13)). The licensee must also be able to show that each administrator meets the certification or recertification rules (section 87412(d)).

Volunteers need a health statement, their health screening documents, and the same criminal record papers if they must be fingerprinted (section 87412(b)).

RCFE: training records

The file has to prove the training happened. Under section 87412(c)(2), each training record shows:

  • the trainer's full name,
  • the subjects covered,
  • the dates of attendance, and
  • the number of hours for each subject.

Classroom training is shown by a notice of completion signed by the trainer. On-the-job training needs a statement from the trainer saying what was covered (section 87411(c)(6)).

The hours. For staff who help residents with personal activities of daily living, Health and Safety Code 1569.625 sets:

  • 40 hours of initial training. 20 hours before the person works independently with residents, including 6 on dementia care and 4 on postural supports, restricted health conditions and hospice care. The other 20 hours, including 6 more on dementia care, within the first four weeks.
  • 20 hours every year after that, including 8 on dementia care and 4 on postural supports, restricted health conditions and hospice care.
  • The statute also sets 16 hours of hands-on training. How those hours are counted within the 40 is a common question. Ask your training vendor or Regional Office before you plan a new hire's first month.

Registered nurses, licensed vocational nurses and certified nurse assistants follow different rules under subdivision (d) of the same section.

Staff who help residents take their own medications have a separate requirement under Health and Safety Code 1569.69: 10 hours of initial training in a home licensed for 15 or fewer (6 of them hands-on shadowing), or 24 hours in a home licensed for 16 or more (16 of them hands-on shadowing), a passed exam, and 8 hours of in-service training every 12 months. The section also sets how soon the initial training must be finished; read it for your home's size.

A warning about old numbers. As of October 2026, the regulation text at section 87412(c)(1)(A) still describes 10 hours of initial training and 4 hours a year. The statute above has required 40 and 20 since January 1, 2016. Plan to the statute's numbers, and ask your Regional Office if a checklist you were given shows the older ones.

ARF: what goes in each staff file

Section 80066(a) lists twelve items for the licensee, the administrator and each employee:

  • Full name
  • Driver's license number, if the person will transport clients
  • Date of employment
  • A statement signed by the employee that they are at least 18
  • Home address and phone number
  • Documentation of the education, training or experience the rules require for your facility type
  • Past experience, including the types of work and former employers
  • The person's duties
  • Termination date, if the person no longer works there
  • A health screening (see the timing below)
  • Tuberculosis test documents
  • For anyone who must be fingerprinted: a signed statement about their criminal record history, and proof of a criminal record clearance or exemption

As with RCFEs, a certified administrator's current, valid certificate meets the criminal record item.

Volunteers need a health statement, tuberculosis test documents, and the same criminal record papers if they must be fingerprinted (section 80066(b)).

On-the-job training is part of the ARF file too. Section 80065(f) says every person needs on-the-job training or related experience for their job in: nutrition and food handling, housekeeping and sanitation, client care and supervision, assisting with self-administered medications, recognizing early signs of illness, and community services and resources. Staff must also be told to report any violation of clients' personal rights (section 80065(m)).

CPR is not in this table because the three general sections above do not name it. Other ARF rules can require more training for particular programs, so ask your Regional Office what applies to yours.

Keeping the files

  • At the home. Records are kept at the facility. A central office is allowed only if the records are still readily available at the home (sections 87412(g) and 80066(e)).
  • Open to Licensing. The licensing agency can inspect, audit and copy personnel records on demand during normal business hours (sections 87412(f) and 80066(c)).
  • For three years. Keep each file at least three years after the person's employment ends (sections 87412(h) and 80066(d)).
  • With hours worked. Records must show the hours each person actually worked (sections 87412(e) and 80066(f)).

Forms CDSS publishes for this

The two personnel records sections above do not name a form. CDSS publishes forms that hold the same information, on its forms list:

  • LIC 501, Personnel Record
  • LIC 503, Health Screening Report - Facility Personnel
  • LIC 500, Personnel Report
  • LIC 9163, Request Live Scan Service - Community Care Licensing

Check the list for the current revision before you print a stack.
