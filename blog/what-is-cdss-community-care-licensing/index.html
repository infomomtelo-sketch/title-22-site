What Is CDSS Community Care Licensing? A Plain Guide for California Care Homes

October 2026 · CDSS Licensing basics · By the Title22 team

If you run a care home in California, or you are looking for one for a parent, you will hear "DSS," "CCL" and "Title 22" in almost every conversation. This guide explains who these people are, what they do, and where to find the official answers yourself.

CDSS, CCLD and "Licensing" are the same family

CDSS is the California Department of Social Services. Inside it, the Community Care Licensing Division (CCLD) licenses and monitors community care. When an administrator says "Licensing is coming," this is who they mean. CCLD says its mission is to "promote the health, safety, and quality of life of each person in community care" through a regulatory enforcement system (CCLD home page).

CCLD is organized into program areas: Adult Care, Senior Care, Child Care, Children's Residential, Continuing Care and Home Care Services. Care homes for adults and seniors fall under the Adult and Senior Care Program, which covers:

  • Residential Care Facilities for the Elderly (RCFEs), often called assisted living or board and care, mostly for people 60 and older.
  • Adult Residential Facilities (ARFs), for adults 18 through 59 who need care and supervision.
  • Adult day programs and several smaller facility types.

Not sure which one you are? Read RCFE vs ARF: what's the difference.

Nonmedical care is the key idea

Community care facilities provide nonmedical care and supervision. That is how Title 22 defines them (Title 22, section 80001). Help with bathing, dressing, meals, medication reminders and assisted self-administration is community care. Skilled nursing homes and hospitals are a different system, licensed by the California Department of Public Health, not CDSS.

Who you actually deal with

  • Your Regional Office. Each facility is assigned to a local CCLD office. The Adult and Senior Care offices are listed in the regional office directory (PDF).
  • Your Licensing Program Analyst (LPA). The analyst who inspects your home. For adult and senior care, analysts use CCLD's CARE Tools, standard checklists for pre-licensing and routine inspections. Reading them is one of the best ways to see what an analyst will look at.
  • The Administrator Certification Bureau (ACB). Issues and renews administrator certificates and approves training vendors. See our guide to the ICTP and the 40-hour renewal checklist.

How complaints work

Anyone can file a complaint about a licensed facility: a resident, a family member, a staff member or a neighbor. Per the CCLD Complaint Hotline page:

  • Call 1-844-538-8766 (844-LET-US-NO), email letusno@dss.ca.gov, or use the online complaint form.
  • The local office makes an unannounced visit within 10 days of receiving the complaint.
  • Findings are substantiated, unsubstantiated or unfounded, judged by the "preponderance of the evidence."
  • The person who complained stays confidential unless they agree otherwise.

How CDSS tells providers about changes

CDSS announces new laws, policy changes and emergencies through Provider Information Notices (PINs). The Adult and Senior Care PINs are listed on the PIN page, and you can subscribe to licensing updates by email. If you only bookmark one thing from this article, make it the PIN page. Most "nobody told us" citations come from a PIN the facility never read.
