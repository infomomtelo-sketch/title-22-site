Who's Who in California's Care-Home Community

October 2026 · Community Resources · By the Title22 team

A California care home sits in the middle of a busy community: regulators, advocates, trainers, families, funders and other operators. Knowing who does what saves time, and it helps you call the right person the first time.

The regulators

  • CDSS Community Care Licensing Division. Licenses and inspects RCFEs, ARFs and other community care (CCLD). Start with our plain guide to CDSS Licensing.
  • Regional Offices and Licensing Program Analysts. Your day-to-day contact for inspections, questions and your facility file (Adult and Senior Care office directory).
  • Administrator Certification Bureau. Administrator certificates and the approved training vendors.

The protectors and advocates

  • Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Run through the California Department of Aging and local programs in each area. Ombudsman representatives help residents with day-to-day care, health, safety, rights and personal preferences, and look into reports of abuse or neglect. Services are free and complaints are confidential. Statewide line: 1-800-231-4024, 24/7 (Ombudsman program).
  • Adult Protective Services (APS). Each county runs APS for adults 60 and older, and for dependent adults 18 to 59, who may be abused, neglected or exploited. Statewide line: 1-833-401-0832 (CDSS APS page).
  • California Advocates for Nursing Home Reform (CANHR). A nonprofit advocacy group that publishes consumer guides on assisted living and RCFEs (canhr.org).

The funders and partners for ARFs

  • Regional centers. California's Department of Developmental Services works through 21 nonprofit regional centers that assess people with developmental disabilities, plan their services and buy them. Many ARF operators work closely with their local regional center.

The trainers

Every administrator certificate starts with an approved Initial Certification Training Program and is renewed with 40 hours of continuing education every two years. Trainers are often the people new administrators trust most. See how to choose a CE course. If you are a trainer, see how Title22 works with trainers.

The operators' groups

  • California Assisted Living Association (CALA). A statewide association for assisted living and RCFE providers (caassistedliving.org).
  • Local operator groups and county networks. Ask your trainer or other administrators which ones meet near you.

Families and residents

Families are part of the community too. Our guide for families shows how to check a home's license and reports. For dementia questions, the Alzheimer's Association runs a 24/7 helpline at 1-800-272-3900.

Staying current

Rules change every year. The simplest habits: read new Adult and Senior Care PINs, subscribe to CDSS licensing updates, and keep this blog bookmarked. We write about changes in plain words.
